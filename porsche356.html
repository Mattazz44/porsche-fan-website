Porsche 356 "No. 1" Roadster

June 8, 1948: The Birth of the Porsche Sports Car

Class: Founding Lightweight Mid-Engine Roadster (Chassis 356-001)

Completed in the Austrian sawmill workshop of Gmund, Carinthia, chassis 356-001 received its general operating permit on June 8, 1948. Designed by Ferry Porsche, Erwin Komenda, and Karl Rabe, the 356 "No. 1" Roadster realized Ferry Porsche's vision of a small, agile, and aerodynamically clean sports car built around lightweight efficiency rather than raw engine size.

"In the beginning I looked around and could not find quite the car I dreamed of. So I decided to build it myself." Ferry Porsche

Unlike later rear-engined series-production 356 coupes, the "No. 1" prototype placed its tuned 1,131 cc air-cooled flat-four ahead of the rear axle inside a tubular steel spaceframe clad in hand-formed aluminum. At just 585 kg, its 35 hp propelled the open roadster to 135 km/h with crisp cornering poise.

First Class Victory & Enduring DNA

Barely a month after road certification, the 356 "No. 1" Roadster won its class at the July 1948 Innsbruck city race, launching a motorsport lineage that now exceeds 30,000 race victories worldwide. Its mid-engine roadster DNA lives directly in today's 718 Spyder RS.
