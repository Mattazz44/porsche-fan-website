Porsche 917K Kurzheck

Air-Cooled Flat-12 Legend & First Outright Le Mans Crown

Class: FIA Group 5 International Championship for Makes

Developed in just ten months under Ferdinand Piech to exploit FIA's 25-car Group 5 sports-car homologation rule, the Porsche 917 combined an ultra-lightweight 42 kg pressurized tubular aluminum spaceframe with Hans Mezger's Type 912 air-cooled 12-cylinder boxer engine.

"Transforming the skittish 1969 long-tail into the short-tail Kurzheck gave drivers the aerodynamic stability to unleash 600 horsepower down the Mulsanne straight."

After severe rear-end lift plagued its 1969 debut, joint testing with John Wyer's JWA Gulf team revealed that truncating and raising the rear deck turned the 917K into an unstoppable endurance weapon capable of 354 km/h while remaining planted through high-speed sweepers.

1970 & 1971 Le Mans Victories

In torrential rain at the 1970 24 Hours of Le Mans, Hans Herrmann and Richard Attwood piloted the red-and-white Salzburg #23 Porsche 917K to Porsche's very first overall Le Mans victory. In 1971, Helmut Marko and Gijs van Lennep repeated the feat in the magnesium-framed #22 Martini 917K, setting a 5,335.313 km distance record that stood for 39 years.
