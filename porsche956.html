Porsche 956 Group C

Ground-Effect Revolution & The 6:11.13 Nordschleife Legend

Class: FIA Group C World Sportscar Championship

Designed under Norbert Singer for the 1982 FIA Group C fuel-consumption formula, the Porsche 956 became Porsche's first race car built around an aluminum monocoque chassis. By sculpting twin venturi tunnels beneath the sidepods, the 956 generated theoretical downforce exceeding its own 820 kg mass at 321.4 km/h.

"With ground-effect suction pinning the 956 to the asphalt, Stefan Bellof averaged more than 202 km/h around the Nurburgring Nordschleife."

Power came from the Type-935/76 2.65-liter twin-turbocharged flat-six featuring air-cooled cylinders and water-cooled four-valve heads, tuned to extract roughly 620 hp while adhering to strict Group C fuel allocation limits. It also served as the early testbed for Porsche's dual-clutch PDK transmission.

Le Mans 1-2-3 Sweep & 35-Year Record

On its 1982 Le Mans debut, factory Rothmans Porsche 956 entries finished 1-2-3 in exact car-number order (#1, #2, #3). On May 28, 1983 during qualifying for the 1000 km of Nurburgring, Stefan Bellof set a 6:11.13 lap around the 20.835 km Nordschleife that stood unbroken for 35 years until the Porsche 919 Hybrid Evo.
