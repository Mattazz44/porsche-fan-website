Porsche 959

Group B Technological Pioneer & Desert Rally Conqueror

Class: Group B Homologation Supercar / AWD Pioneer

Originally conceived to satisfy FIA Group B rally homologation, the Porsche 959 emerged in 1986 as the world's fastest street-legal production car and a decade-ahead showcase of electronic chassis engineering. Its Kevlar-aramid composite body panels and aluminum doors wrapped a zero-lift aerodynamic silhouette capable of 317 km/h.

"Sequential twin-turbocharging, hollow-spoke magnesium wheels with tire-pressure telemetry, and variable torque distribution defined the template for every modern supercar."

Derived from the Group C 956/962 racing block, the 2.85-liter flat-six combined air-cooled cylinders with water-cooled four-valve cylinder heads and sequentially staged turbochargers to eliminate lag. Its Porsche-Steuer Kupplung (PSK) all-wheel-drive system dynamically shifted up to 80 percent of torque rearward under hard acceleration across four driver-selectable terrain programs.

1986 Paris-Dakar 1-2 Victory

Before customer deliveries began, Porsche entered rally-prepared 959 prototypes in the brutal 14,000 km 1986 Paris-Dakar Rally. Rene Metge and Dominique Lemoyne won outright ahead of Jacky Ickx in second, validating the all-wheel-drive architecture that evolved directly into the 911 Carrera 4 and 911 Turbo S.
